Accounts & households

Family Meal Mixer uses one shared login (email + password) per household. We do not currently offer separate accounts, logins, or sign-in for individual family members, including children — any "profile" you create inside your household (a name, an optional PIN, an optional photo) is managed by the adult who controls the household account, not a separate account of its own.

What we collect

Account information: the email address and password you sign up with.

Household & profile information: your household name, and the names, roles, optional PINs, and optional photos of the profiles you create inside it.

Diet & allergy information: any dietary restrictions or allergy keywords you choose to add to a profile, used to shape meal suggestions.

Meal planning content: recipes, ingredients, cooking steps, photos, your weekly meal schedule, pantry contents, and voting/history you create in the app.

Social profile information, if you use it: a public @username, display name, bio, and the recipes and weekly plan you choose to make visible — see "Public social profiles" below for how visibility currently works.

Usage & diagnostic information: app-feature events (for example, that a vote was opened, or a meal was marked as made), counts, and short error/crash details. This is deliberately limited by the app's own code to enums, counts, and IDs — never recipe text, names, emails, or passwords.

Push notification tokens, if you enable notifications, so we can deliver alerts like a vote being open or your weekly plan being ready.

A calendar-feed link, only if you turn that feature on, that lets a calendar app you choose read your dinner schedule.

The anonymous recipe corpus

When you save or import a recipe, the recipe's facts — its title, whether it's a main or a side, its protein, cuisine, and ingredient names — may be added to a shared, anonymous database we use to make suggestions better for every household. This record is not linked to your account, household, or any other household's data in any way our systems retain — no household ID, user ID, or personal note is stored with it. You can turn this off (it shares one setting with usage analytics) in Settings → Privacy & data; turning it off also clears anything still queued to send.

Public social profiles — on by default today

The social/community feature is currently on by default for every household: when your household is created, your profile is public and searchable, your weekly plan is visible to anyone who follows you, and other households can copy your recipes into their own library, unless and until you turn one or more of those off in Settings → Social profile. We are reviewing whether this should default to private instead; until that changes, please check that screen if you don't want your household discoverable.

A household that follows or searches for others can also see recipe photos you uploaded, if your profile is public, through both your profile page and a "popular with other families" suggestions feature.

AI-assisted recipe import

Importing a recipe from a link works by reading that page's own published recipe data and does not involve an AI service.

Importing from a screenshot, a photo, or pasted text — and searching the web for recipes — sends that image or text to a third-party AI provider (Google's Gemini API and/or Anthropic's API, and Brave's Search API for web search) solely to extract or find recipe information. These are Plus features with a monthly usage limit per household. Please don't submit a photo containing anything other than a recipe, ingredient list, or pantry contents.

Who we share information with

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not use it for advertising or ad targeting.

We share information with the service providers that run the app on our behalf, only as needed for them to provide that service: Supabase (our database, authentication, file storage, and backend hosting), Google and/or Anthropic (recipe extraction from a screenshot/photo/text, and web search fallback), Brave (recipe web search), and Expo (push notification delivery and app distribution).

Other households can see information you've chosen to make public through the social profile feature described above.

We may disclose information if required by law, or to protect the rights, safety, or property of our users or the public.

Your choices & rights

You can review and edit most of your information directly in the app: your household name, profiles, diet/allergy settings, recipes, and social profile visibility.

You can opt out of usage analytics and the anonymous recipe corpus at any time in Settings → Privacy & data.

We do not yet have a self-service "delete my account" button in the app. Until that ships, you can request access to, correction of, or deletion of your information by contacting us at [CONTACT_EMAIL], and we will handle it manually. If you are a California or other U.S. state resident, you may have additional rights under your state's privacy law — attorney review of this section is pending, and this Policy will be updated to name those rights specifically before broad launch.

Data retention

We currently keep your account and household data for as long as your account is active. We do not yet have an automated retention/deletion schedule for usage-analytics or anonymous-corpus records; this is in development. Until an automated process exists, you can request deletion by contacting us as described above.

Children

Family Meal Mixer is intended to be used by adults managing a household's meal planning. We do not knowingly collect personal information directly from children, and the app has no separate account, login, or profile type for a child that would let a child create an account on their own. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information outside of a parent-managed household profile, please contact us at [CONTACT_EMAIL].

Security

Your household's data is isolated from every other household's using database access rules enforced on our backend. Profile PINs are stored as salted cryptographic hashes, never in plain text. Data is encrypted in transit. No method of storage or transmission is 100% secure, and we can't guarantee absolute security.
